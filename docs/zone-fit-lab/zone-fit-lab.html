Zones never overlap

Powerbar's three zones share one line, and the line is only as long as the screen. Lasting length makes room: the centre slides off the middle by the least distance that keeps one zone gap, and when that isn't enough the longest zone folds its farthest widgets into its drawer. A moment of length borrows room: a hover reveal, an activity reveal or a drag gap makes the neighbour's nearest widgets yield until it ends. Nothing ever paints over anything else, in every bar style and at any opacity. The preview is your own bar: your layout, captures, theme and wallpaper, at your screen's real length.

    What ships

    Always on; there is no setting. Zones that fit are laid out exactly as the spacing spec says, with the centre on the middle.

    • Zone gap g = C + s

      One icon cell plus the user's spacing between any two zones, in every style. Islands use the same gap.

    • Slide, then fold

      The centre slides even when an anchor is set; folding starts only when sliding can't fit. The longest zone folds first, from the end farthest from its anchor.

    • Dots with a count, wherever widgets are out of view

      Three dots along the bar (󰇙 on vertical bars, 󰇘 on horizontal ones) with the shared count badge. A folded zone's dots are the drawer handle: hovering or focusing them opens the zone's drawer, folded widgets first. A yielding zone's dots are quiet (45%) and not interactive.

    • Moments yield, with a smaller gap

      Hover reveals, activity reveals and drag gaps never slide anything; the neighbour's nearest whole widgets fade out until the moment ends. Moments keep the 4 px edge gap instead of the zone gap, and the yielding zone's marker takes the first freed cell, so it always has room (decided 2 October, after the first live run).

    • Anchors stay

      The centre's anchored widget and each side zone's outermost widget never fold or yield. A moment that would need them, that doesn't fit on the screen, or whose widget is folded, opens in the zone's drawer.

    • One quiet line in Preferences

      On the Layout page, under the preview, when anything is folded: "3 widgets don't fit on eDP-1. They're in the Center drawer."

    The rule

    Three steps, tried in order. Each step is cheaper for the eye than the next, and nothing is ever hidden without a way to reach it.

    1. Slide.

      If the zones fit end to end, the centre moves the least distance that clears its neighbours by one zone gap. The anchored widget stays as close to the middle as the space allows. GNOME Shell, GTK's centre box and Waybar all do this.

    2. Fold.

      If even that doesn't fit, the longest zone folds the widget farthest from its anchor (the screen edge, or the centre anchor) into its drawer, one at a time. A handle with a count takes the folded end, so the widgets stay one hover away. This is the overflow menu of macOS 27, Windows 11 and Material.

    3. Yield, for a moment.

      Hover reveals, activity reveals and drag gaps last seconds. For those the growing zone keeps its place and nothing slides; the neighbour's widgets that would be covered fade out, whole, and come back when the moment ends; quiet dots with their count mark where they went. iOS hides status items the same way while the Dynamic Island expands.

    Why the moment never slides anything. Sliding the other zone moves the clock, the one widget everyone glances at, twice in a few seconds; sliding the growing zone moves targets under a resting pointer. Fading whole widgets moves nothing.

    Why whole widgets. A half-covered icon reads as a rendering bug, and its hit area is ambiguous. Yielding and folding work in whole widgets, so every glyph you see is complete and clickable.

    Why a zone never dims beneath another

    A dimmed zone only reads as "beneath" where a solid surface covers it. Measured as how strongly a dimmed glyph shows through the surface in front, against the 13% hover fill: anything stronger than a hover reads as clutter.

    BarSurface in frontShows throughReads as

    Dim strength d = 35%, the level that still reads as "present but inactive". Flat and Floating bars draw one surface for all three zones, so there is no surface in front of the dimmed zone. At 100% the dimmed widgets vanish under the island and the option becomes "hide", except for the parts that stick out past the island's end.

    How zones stay apart on a transparent bar

    With a low background opacity, or on a Flat bar, zones have no visible edges; only air tells one zone from the next. The zone gap g = C + s keeps the air between zones at least twice the air between widgets in every mode and size.

    BarSpacingAir between widgetsAir between zonesRatio

    Proximity does the grouping. People read groups from the ratio of space between groups to space within them; about 2× reads clearly. One icon cell C is the smallest unit the bar already uses, so it needs no new token, and adding the spacing keeps the ratio when the user widens gaps.

    Yield fades to zero. On a transparent bar a half-faded widget has nothing to hide behind, so yielding always ends at 0% and the island end (if any) retracts with it. There is never a frame where two zones draw in one place.

    Handles use the bar's own foreground. The fold handle and its count follow barForeground, which already switches to the wallpaper-aware colour when Omarchy's transparent bar is on. Drawers keep their readable minimum (AppearanceModel.overlayOpacity, 88%).

    Motion

    One switch, powerbarAnimations (Preferences, General, "Animations"). Motion never carries the meaning alone; with animations off every change lands in the same frame, and the end state says the same thing.

    1. Yield first, then grow.

      The neighbour fades out in 80 ms before the growing zone moves, so no frame shows two zones in one place; growth follows in 180 ms with a strong ease-out. The whole open takes 260 ms. Closing reverses it: the zone shrinks in 150 ms, then the yielded widgets fade back in 160 ms.

    2. Slides are rare and slow enough to follow.

      The centre slides only when lasting content changes length, in 200 ms with ease-in-out, because it moves on screen. Widths come from the width hold (widthHoldMs, 8 s), so a ticking clock never nudges it.

    3. Folding swaps in place.

      The folded widget fades in 110 ms while the handle fades and scales up from 0.96 in 160 ms; its neighbours close the gap in 150 ms.

    4. Off means idle.

      No per-frame work. The solver runs on layout changes only, and with animations off the yield, growth, slide and fold happen in one binding update.

    How it maps onto Powerbar

    The target state, in the order it can land. The full plan, with files, tests and risks, is docs/zone-fit-implementation.md.

    1. ZoneFitModel.js solves each bar's line.

      fit({ length, inset, gap, mode, zones, transient }) returns { centerStart, folded, yielded, handles, fallback }. Pure, with node tests: no shared pixels, gap kept, anchors kept, moments never move the anchor, unfolding needs a gutter of slack.

    2. Slots report extents, not positions.

      Each zone's visible slots in order, with cellExtent (held widths) and cellSpill; zone length adds CellModel.endPad at both ends. Zone gap cellTokens.cell + widgetSpacing.

    3. The centre is placed by x/y.

      groupModules and centerAnchorModule take their along-axis position from centerStart; no anchors switched by orientation.

    4. Folded slots live in the drawer.

      mainVisible is false for a folded slot, managedSlots(zone) includes it (first), and the zone's 󰇘 handle sits inside the island at the folded end with the count badge.

    5. Moments yield.

      centerSectionRevealHeld, activity reveals (activityVisible) and drag gaps (dragPreview) are the transient input; yielded slots keep their space, fade to 0, lose enabled and focus, and the island ends at the last visible slot.

    6. Motion follows animationsEnabled.

      Yield, then grow; shrink, then return; with animations off, one binding update.

    What the research says

    The references the design borrows from, and the part each one contributed.

    • GTK 4, GtkCenterBoxKeeps the middle child centred as well as possible and pushes it sideways rather than overlap; shrink-center-last decides who gives up space first.
    • GNOME Shell, panel.jsThe top bar's allocation caps the side boxes and shifts the centre box when they need room: slide before anything else.
    • Waybar and Polybar #683fixed-center keeps the centre in the middle "whenever possible" but lets other blocks push it; Polybar's absolute centre is where overlaps come from.
    • Apple HIG, the menu barWhen space is short the system hides menu bar extras to make room for menus and essential extras: a priority order, decided by the system.
    • macOS 27 menu bar overflowItems that don't fit are no longer lost: an arrow at the edge of the icon area expands them in the bar. Hidden items need a visible way back.
    • Windows 11 taskbar overflowA ⋯ button opens a flyout with the icons that didn't fit; the newer build first scales icons down.
    • Android status bar overflow dotA single dot stands for icons that didn't fit, on the side where they were: the handle sits where the content went.
    • Material 3 top app barActions move into the overflow menu from the far end first, so the most important one is the last to go: fold from the end farthest from the anchor.
    • Dynamic Island and Apple developer forumsWhile a Live Activity occupies the island, iOS hides the status bar's breadcrumb and brings it back after: temporary growth borrows room, and the borrowed item returns.
    • Meet Liquid Glass (WWDC25)Content under a glass bar is blurred and faded by a scroll edge effect, and dimmed on dark content: "beneath" only reads with a surface that hides it.
    • Gestalt principles of grouping and LogRocket on proximityGroups read from the ratio of space between groups to space within them; the zone gap keeps it at 2× or more.
    • WebAIM, contrastText over translucent surfaces and images needs its lowest-contrast spot checked; a dimmed glyph behind a 13% surface sits in exactly that spot.
