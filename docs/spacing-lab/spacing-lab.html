One cell, one inset

Powerbar's spacing and highlight specification. Every widget's layout box is also its highlight: icons share one cell, text gets the same air an icon has, and every gap, island end and edge uses the same 3 px inset, so highlights are concentric with the island. Two modes build on it, Equal slots and Equal gaps. Hover or Tab through the bar: the widgets are your own cached captures, on your current wallpaper and theme.

Two spacing options, one highlight

Both options ship and become a choice in Preferences. They share the highlight: the cell's box, centred on the glyph. "One cell, one inset" stays the name of the design idea; users see plainer names.

  • Equal slots is the one cell, one inset rule and the default.

    Every glyph gets the same square slot, and highlights sit one inset apart. At spacing 0 the icon pitch is 23 px on the standard bar and 35 px on the large bar; Space between widgets 4 gives the standard bar Omarchy's 27 px icon pitch. Corrected on 30 September: icon cells were 24 × 20 on the standard bar, which broke the circles this page describes.

  • Equal gaps puts the same air between every pair of glyphs.

    Highlights stay symmetric and may overlap in the gaps. Where two overlap, the older one fades to 45%.

  • Every animation follows one switch.

    Preferences → Behavior → Animations (powerbarAnimations). With it off, everything still works and still reads clearly: each change lands in its final state at once, and the bar does no per-frame work.

  • Icon cells are square on every bar.

    C = T, so an icon's highlight is a square, and a circle wherever the island is a capsule. Space between widgets widens gutters and gaps, never cells.

  • Vertical bars use the same rule turned on its side.

    The theme's vertical size (28 px, 40 px large), cells measured along the bar, glyphs centred on the bar's centre line by their ink, nothing wider than T across the bar, badges 1 px inside the island.

  • Extended islands are islands.

    Strips follow the island rule along the bar's axis, one bar gap from the bar, centred on the group icon. Menus stack highlight rows with the island's radius: icons keep their cell, names start after it or at the icon edge, values and counts trail. They line up with the icon that opened them and grow out of it.

  • In Preferences it is a two-chip row called Spacing in the Style section, above the existing "Space between widgets" slider.

    Config key powerbarSpacingMode: "slots" (default) or "gaps". Each option's one-line description replaces the row's description when chosen, the way PrefsChoice already works.

The rule

Six numbers, all derived from the bar's thickness and the theme. Values update with the controls above.

Why the box and the highlight are the same thing. Layout comes from the glyph's measured ink, never from the slot a widget asks for, and the highlight is that layout box. Highlights therefore fit their pitch exactly, sit centred on what is painted, and meet the island's ends concentrically without any adjustment.

Two modes, one highlight. Both modes draw the cell's box centred on the glyph. Equal slots puts the boxes one inset apart, which gives a fixed pitch: highlights never overlap, hit targets are all the same size, and changing the icon font never reflows the island. Equal gaps puts the same air between every pair of glyphs and lets the box spill into the gaps, which looks calmer when glyph widths vary a lot. A later section shows why the spill never reaches a neighbour's glyph.

Why one inset everywhere. Concentric corners need the gap between an inner shape and its container to equal the difference of their radii. Using the same 3 px (plus any Space between widgets, which only widens gutters) for the gutter between cells, the island's ends and the bar's top and bottom makes every highlight nest inside the island with a parallel curve, and makes a row of highlights tile like a segmented control.

The two modes side by side

Your right island on the large bar, every highlight shown at once, with the measured air between glyphs underneath. With every item lit, Equal gaps shows where neighbouring cells overlap in the gaps; in use you almost always see one highlight at a time.

Why equal-gap highlights can overflow

Equal gaps keeps the same air between glyphs and draws each highlight as the symmetric cell box, even where it spills past the glyph into the gap. The gap is sized so the spill never reaches the next widget's pixels.

spill = (C − ink) / 2glyph

How far a glyph's highlight reaches past its ink on each side. Largest for the narrowest glyph.

gap G = C / 2 + 2 + spacing

Half an icon cell plus 2 px, plus Space between widgets. The spill is below C / 2 for any glyph, so G always covers it.

clearance = G − spill = 2 + spacing + ink / 2≥ 2 px

Distance from a highlight's end to the next widget's glyph. It doesn't depend on bar size. For text it is G − a = 8 px plus spacing.

island end = i + spillconcentric

Measure the ends from the first and last highlight, not the glyph, so those highlights sit exactly one inset from the island's curve.

overlap = spill₁ + spill₂ − G

Two neighbouring highlights overlap in the gap. They stay symmetric anyway: one highlight at a time is by far the common case, and a trimmed shape would look wrong exactly when two show. The more recently lit one draws on top at full strength; the older one fades to 45%.

Dragging widgets of any width

Drag the lifted widget along the island and let go to see it settle. From the keyboard: Space picks it up or drops it, ← and → move it, Esc puts it back. Try the clock over icons and an icon over the Audio group: that mix of sizes is where drag and drop usually breaks.

Dragging
Spacing
  1. The gap is the dragged widget's own landing size.

    Equal slots: its cell plus one inset. Equal gaps: its glyph plus one gap. A widget's own margins never leak in. Reordering inside an island never changes its length, because the source closes by exactly what the gap opens.

  2. The placeholder is the widget's highlight, outlined.

    It has the dragged widget's length, thickness and radius, where it will land. In Equal gaps it spills into the gaps like any highlight, staying 8 px clear of text and at least 2 px clear of glyphs.

  3. Land at the spot nearest the ghost's centre.

    Measured on the resting layout (as if the gap were not there), with a 3 px dead zone. The landing spot depends only on where the ghost is, so it cannot flicker even when the gap is wider than its neighbours. The ghost is never more than half a neighbour's cell from where it will land. Using the ghost's centre rather than the pointer means a wide widget grabbed at one end lands where it looks.

  4. Scale the timing with distance.

    150 ms × √(gap ÷ icon gap), capped at 260 ms, on an ease-in-out sine. Icons take 150 ms; the clock's 130 px gap takes 260 ms, and its neighbours peak at 2.2× an icon's speed, where a fixed duration would reach 3.7×.

  5. Everything that moves shares that duration.

    Neighbours, the placeholder and the island's growing end all use the same timing, so the island's curve and its last cell stay concentric throughout.

  6. Settle on release.

    In one 180 ms strong ease-out, the ghost glides its glyph centre onto the landing cell's centre, drops its 2 px lift and 1.04 scale, and fades its surface and shadow, while the placeholder outline fades out. What remains is an ordinary widget in the island; nothing else moves. Picking it up again lifts it in 120 ms. Esc settles it back where it was lifted.

With "Landing boundaries" on, the ticks under the island are the points the ghost's centre (the dot) must cross to move the gap. They sit halfway between neighbouring landing spots, measured without the gap, so they never move while you drag. Peak speed of a neighbour on the ease-in-out sine is π × distance ÷ (2 × duration). Ratios compare with an icon's 35 px gap at 150 ms. Vertical bars

The same rule turned on its side. Cells grow along the bar and never across it, highlights are the cell box in both spacing modes, and glyphs centre on the bar's centre line. Your left, centre and right islands are drawn side by side; hover any of them.

Along the bar, nothing changes. An icon's cell is C long, text is its ink plus the air a at each end, gutters and island ends are one inset, and Equal gaps keeps G between glyphs with the highlight spilling symmetrically. Both modes reuse the horizontal layout code on the other axis, so a rule can't drift between orientations.

Across the bar, centre the ink. A widget captured for a horizontal bar has a slot shaped for that bar: the tray's chevron sits 14 px right of its slot's centre, System update's 9 px glyph sits in a 38 px slot. On a vertical bar those slots mean nothing, so every glyph centres on the bar's centre line by its ink.

The bar never widens. Content across the bar must fit the highlight's thickness T. Widgets bring a vertical form (the clock's HH / — / mm, workspaces as a column), and a group whose face is wider than T shows its glyph.

Along the bar, measure the glyph, not its canvas. An icon's ink along a vertical bar is its glyph's painted height, measured from the glyph text, not the 16–28 px canvas the kit draws it in; otherwise Equal gaps runs 4–16 px sparser than a horizontal bar. A vertical form should keep its horizontal pitch: Omarchy's built-in Workspaces and clock still give each item a full bar-size slot (about 40 px and 27 px), which is theirs to tighten.

Bar

Glyphs are your captures; the AI face, workspace numbers, the clock and EN are their vertical forms drawn in the bar font. The AI group's drawer is open, so it shows the open fill. Tab walks the cells; j and k walk the workspaces.

Extended islands

Group drawers and managed drawers are islands too. They are built from the same cells, sit one bar gap from the island that opened them, and line up with the icon that opened them. Icons only gives a strip that follows the bar; names only and icons and names give a menu.

A drawer is a second row of the bar. The strip is an island made by the main rule: the bar's thickness, cells, gutters, ends, radius and spacing mode, along the bar's own axis. It sits one bar gap (the same 4 px as between the bar and the screen edge) from the island, so the two read as related surfaces rather than one merged shape.

A menu is the same cells stacked. Rows are highlight cells, one inset apart and one inset from the menu's edge, so the menu takes the island's radius and every row corner is concentric with it. The layout is the same in both spacing modes: a menu has no row of glyphs to space.

Three columns, each with one rule. An icon keeps its bar cell at the row's start, so its glyph sits exactly where a strip's first glyph would; switching between Icons and Icons and names leaves every glyph where it was. Names start where that cell ends. Anything that is text on the bar, like the clock, is a value: values and counts trail at the row's end, and a row without an icon starts its name at the icon edge, so the menu's content has one left edge.

It starts where you pointed. Below a horizontal bar the first row starts where the group icon's highlight starts, so a group glyph sits directly above its first member's glyph. Beside a vertical bar the first row sits level with the icon. Near a screen edge the menu flips.

Extended island edge cases

Each case shows Equal slots above Equal gaps, on the large bar.

  • Many members.

    A strip longer than the screen, less a bar gap at each end, scrolls along its axis with an 8 px fade at the clipped end; a menu taller than the space below the bar scrolls vertically. The keyboard cursor scrolls its row into view.

  • A member whose widget is gone.

    It is skipped and leaves no hole; the group keeps the reference so the widget returns to its place if reinstalled.

  • Bottom bar.

    The drawer opens above the bar with the same gap and alignment. Menus keep reading top to bottom and anchor their bottom edge.

  • Two members with one name.

    Later ones get a number ("Clock 2") so menu rows stay distinguishable.

  • Getting there.

    The drawer's pointer area spans the gap to the bar (dashed in "Boxes and gaps"), so moving from the icon to the drawer never crosses dead space; the 450 ms leave grace still applies.

  • Keyboard.

    Strips move with the bar's arrows (h/l or j/k on a vertical bar); menus with j/k. Enter activates, Esc closes and returns focus to the group icon. Opening from the keyboard leaves no row selected until you move.

  • Opening and closing.

    160 ms in, 110 ms out, fading and growing from 94% out of the source cell's centre on the drawer's near edge, below a horizontal bar or beside a vertical one. With Animations off the drawer appears and disappears at once, with the same hover delays; click the group icon in the demo to try both.

  • Flat and floating bars.

    Drawers always float, so they always take the island radius and the bar gap, whatever the bar style.

Highlight states

One shape, one language: every state fills the same cell in the bar's own foreground or the theme's accent. Only focus adds a ring, because keyboard focus must be visible even on top of another state.

Motion

Hover happens tens of times a day, so it barely moves. Things that open or rearrange get just enough motion to explain where they went. All of it is optional: Omarchy has to work well on modest hardware, so with Animations off the bar keeps every function and every cue, and does no per-frame work.

  1. One switch.

    powerbarAnimations, shown as Preferences → Behavior → Animations and read as animationsEnabled. Every Behavior has enabled: animationsEnabled; every explicit animation checks it before starting and, when off, applies its end state directly. There is no second, per-feature switch.

  2. Motion never carries information alone.

    Each animated change ends in a static state that says the same thing: a fill, an outline, a placeholder, a position. Turning motion off removes the travel, never the meaning.

  3. Logic does not depend on motion.

    The 8 s width hold, the drag landing rule, highlight recency and the 2.5 s activity peek run the same with Animations off.

  4. Sequences short-circuit.

    Anything that waits for an animation, such as a drop that glides before it commits, commits in the same event when Animations is off.

  5. Off means idle.

    No FrameAnimation, animation timers or per-frame geometry run with Animations off; highlights re-place when geometry changes. With Animations on, per-frame work runs only while something is actually moving.

Implementing the specification

A sequence, in the order it can land without breaking drag and drop or the drawers. docs/spacing-implementation.md expands each step with files, tests and acceptance checks.

  1. Add CellModel.js with node tests.

    cellLength({ kind, ink, thickness, maxInk }) returns the box length (T for glyphs); layoutZone(items, { inset, spacing, mode }) returns boxes, ink offsets and the island length. Kinds: glyph (has glyphPaintedWidth), text (has labelWidth or any other ink), compound (two or more registered click targets inside the slot).

  2. Measure an ink rectangle, not a width.

    Extend HighlightModel.inkWidth to inkRect returning start and length along either axis, so off-centre content (the tray, widgets with trailing gaps like Workspaces' 1.5 px) centres on what is painted. Kit hints come first (glyphPaintedWidth, labelWidth); otherwise take the span of each painted child's own ink. Only what is visible counts: cut a child's ink to its box when it clips (the tray's closed drawer, hidden indicators), and skip a plain fill covering the widget when it has other content (a plugin's own tint or pill). Ink of at most 16 px reads as an icon, even from the kit's icon button, which can carry a whole string such as 100% 󰁹.

  3. Size slots from the cell.

    In Bar.qml the slot's naturalWidth becomes the cell length and presentation.x shifts by (L − ink) / 2 − inkLeft. Row/Column spacing becomes the gutter (inset plus Space between widgets) and island ends are the inset, with no landing lip during drags: the outlined placeholder shows where a widget lands. The drag gap is the dragged cell plus the gutter, so the ghost's landing slot matches exactly. Across a horizontal bar each slot is at least the bar's thickness with the widget's box centred in it, so a widget shorter than the bar never sits at the top. Position anything that changes with orientation by x/y, never by anchors switched with bindings: a switch passes through a doubly anchored state, and Qt then writes width or height directly, which removes their bindings.

  4. Make the highlight the slot box.

    highlightGeometry reduces to the slot's rectangle at the inset thickness with radius clamp(islandRounding − inset, 0, T / 2). Highlights need no end adjustment: the ends are concentric by construction.

  5. Keep hit areas generous.

    Each slot's pointer area covers its cell plus half of each gutter and the full bar thickness; the first and last cells extend to the island ends (to the screen edge on a flat bar). Clip each slot to that area so oversized widgets cannot steal clicks from neighbours.

  6. Give compounds thumbs.

    For compounds, hovering draws a faint track over the whole cell and a thumb over the hovered click target: side min(T, pitch − 1), radius r − (T − side) / 2. Workspaces and Indicators already register their buttons as click targets.

  7. Hold widths.

    A cell grows at once (150 ms) and shrinks only after 8 s at the smaller size, unless it loses at least one icon cell: then something went away (a reveal closing, a tray item leaving) and it shrinks at once. Widgets that animate their own width (the tray, Indicators) opt out with powerbarWidthHold: false, and a change of orientation, bar size or spacing starts every cell fresh. Cap free text at 160 px of ink with a fade.

  8. Both modes.

    layoutZone(cells, { mode }) with "slots" (cells one inset apart) or "gaps" (glyphs C / 2 + 2 apart, island ends at i + spill), read from powerbarSpacingMode. Both use the same highlight box. In gaps mode highlights may overlap: each HoverHighlight records when it lit, and where two overlap the newer gets the higher z and the older drops to 45% opacity. Preferences shows the choice as a PrefsChoice row, Spacing: Equal slots or Equal gaps.

  9. Drag with landing-size gaps.

    The drag gap is the dragged widget's landing size, the placeholder is its highlight outlined, the landing spot is the one nearest the ghost's centre on the resting layout (3 px dead zone), and dragMotionDuration becomes clamp(150 × √(gap ÷ icon gap), 150, 260). On release the ghost settles onto the cell in 180 ms before the layout commits.

  10. Redefine the Spacing setting.

    Extra spacing widens the gutters between cells (and the gap in Equal gaps), never the cells, so icon highlights stay square and island ends stay concentric; hit areas still split each gutter, so targets grow with the space. At spacing 0 the icon pitch is 23 px on the standard bar and 35 px on the large bar.

  11. Lay vertical bars out with the same model.

    Call layoutZone with along-axis measurements (ink top and height from inkRect) and B = Style.bar.sizeVertical plus the large lip; an icon's along-axis ink is its glyph's painted height, measured from its text with TextMetrics (the kit exposes only the width). Never cap a vertical stack or a compound at the free-text limit. Centre every glyph across the bar by its ink, not its slot; require content across to fit T (widgets supply a vertical form, group faces fall back to their glyph); clamp badges 1 px inside the island on both axes.

  12. Build extended islands from the same model.

    In ManagedDrawer.qml, lay strips out with layoutZone in the current spacing mode at the bar's thickness, radius islandRounding and one-inset ends, along the bar's axis; centre them on the source cell, clamp one edgeGap from the screen edges, then snap an end within T / 2 of the island's end. Place drawers one bar gap from the bar with a pointer area spanning the gap. Menus stack T-tall rows one inset apart with one-inset padding and radius islandRounding; names start at C after an icon (glyph centred in the first C) and at air in rows without one (names only, or a value row), so every row's content shares one left edge; members that are not glyphs show their native look right-aligned at air from the row's end, and counts use the shared badge there. Below a horizontal bar the first row starts at the source highlight's start (or ends at its end); beside a vertical bar the first row's centre is level with the source cell's centre (or the last row's). Show a widget's count with the shared badge rather than letting it paint one over its glyph, scale from the source cell's centre, and use the bar's focus ring for the keyboard cursor.

  13. Put every animation behind the one switch.

    Give every Behavior and ColorAnimation enabled: animationsEnabled, including the bar's colour crossfade, the move-candidate edge, BarBackdrop's fade, Active window's width, the Tray reveal and the Layout editor's tile colour. Run the highlights' FrameAnimation only while their slot is moving and animations are on; otherwise re-place on geometry changes. Add a QML test that turns the flag off and checks each change reaches its end state in the same frame.

  14. Verify on the desktop.

    Tests for cellLength at both bar sizes, glyph, short text, long text and compound kinds; layoutZone ends at the inset; inkRect for off-centre children. Then screenshot all three bar styles, both sizes and a vertical bar with autohide off, once with Animations on and once off, and check the shell stays idle (no per-frame work) with a highlight showing and Animations off.

What the research says

The references this rule borrows from, and the part each one contributed.

  • Material 3 menusMenus as stacked rows with container padding, a leading icon column and a fixed icon-to-label gap; labels align in one column.
  • Apple, Adopt the new look of macOS (WWDC20)Rounded menus with inset, rounded selections: the row highlight sits inside the menu's padding.
  • Ice, the Ice BarHidden menu bar items shown as a second bar under the first: an overflow strip reads as more bar, not as a menu.
  • Smashing, safe triangles and Amazon's dropdownThe path from trigger to menu must be forgiving; no dead space between them.
  • Floating UI, offsetThe distance between an anchor and its floating surface is one gap along the main axis, with separate alignment on the cross axis.
  • Apple, Get to know the new design system (WWDC25)Concentricity: nested shapes share a centre, capsules nest in capsules, grouped toolbar items share one glass surface.
  • Perfect nested border radius and UX Planet on nested cornersInner radius equals outer radius minus the gap; equal radii make the gap look uneven.
  • GNOME Shell panel buttons-natural-hpadding: 12px, -minimum-hpadding: 6px: highlight padding that shrinks when the panel is crowded, so panel buttons are wider than tall.
  • macOS menu bar spacing and IcemacOS separates NSStatusItemSpacing (gap) from NSStatusItemSelectionPadding (highlight padding); padding about twice the spacing looks best, which is the same idea as wide cells and small gutters.
  • Material 3 toolbars and icon button accessibilityA 24 dp icon inside a 48 dp target; the padding is the target. Icon buttons in bars get a container fill.
  • NN/g on Fitts's law and Coding Horror, infinite widthTargets at a screen edge are effectively infinite; a bar should have no dead pixels between items or at its ends.
  • Shestopalov, Optical effects in user interfacesMathematically equal spacing can still look uneven; centre shapes on their visual mass, not their box.
  • Figma auto layoutFixed gap versus space-between versus evenly; fixed gap with padded children is the only one stable under changing content.
