Locked, 4 October 2026: Shelves

Layout as three shelves of your bar

The Layout page is the last part of Preferences still in the old language: a matrix of zones by visibility, a wallpaper strip that redraws your bar at 60–72% of its size, and an inspector that keeps 300 px of the window for three settings. The proposal turns it into tiles like the Bar page: one shelf per state (On the bar, On demand, Hidden), each with your three zones side by side, widgets at their true size, and the selected widget's settings in a tile below. Every widget here is a capture from your own bar, in your layout and theme.

The rule

The page reuses the Bar page's tiles and tokens. Two things are new: shelves that keep their zones in columns, and one widget drawing at one size wherever the bar draws it.

Why shelves. Ice and Bartender, the two tools people use to arrange a menu bar, both show one row per state (Visible, Hidden, Always hidden) and let you drag between them. A shelf reads like the bar it describes: the top zone on the left, the center in the middle, the bottom zone on the right, in the order the bar uses. Tab steps from shelf to shelf, the change people make most after reordering.

Why true size. Today the On the bar row draws your bar inside a 223 px cell per zone, so your Top zone shrinks to 60% and its 38 px icons to 23 px. Without the inspector and the row titles, a zone column is 357 px and your widest zone needs 309 px, so every widget is drawn at the size it has on the bar.

Why no wallpaper. The Bar page dropped its preview because the real bar already shows every change. The Layout page has the same two copies of the bar's look: the wallpaper strip with island surfaces behind the On the bar row, and a second one in the inspector. Widgets keep their real faces, which is how you recognise them, but not the bar's surface.

Why a tile for the selection. The inspector is 300 px wide at all times, and two of its four settings (Zone, Visibility) repeat where the widget already sits. A full-width tile below the shelves holds the same settings in 143 px of height, and keeps Zone and Visibility as the way to move a widget without dragging: Atlassian's user testing found that a menu of move actions is more accessible than arrow-key dragging alone.

Motion

Layout is opened now and then, but a visit is a run of moves, so the motion stays short and nothing moves on its own.

  1. One switch.

    Everything follows powerbarAnimations (Animations, on the Bar page), read as bar.animationsEnabled. Today's editor already honours it for every animation it has (cell height, tile press, tile fill, the wallpaper fade).

  2. Motion never carries information alone.

    The landing place is an outline and the selection is a fill; both are there with Animations off.

  3. Off means idle.

    Nothing on the page runs per frame. With no wallpaper strip, opening the page loads no image beyond the widget captures.

How it maps onto Powerbar

In the order it can land. Each step leaves Preferences working.

  1. Put the editor in a PrefsForm with a PrefsBento.

    Three PrefsTiles (On the bar, On demand, Hidden), span 12 at every column count, gap prefs.tileGap. The header caption comes from the tile's new caption property: Always visible; the on-demand rule from bar.onDemandInline; Kept off the bar. The zone-fit notice (ZoneFitNotice.lines) replaces the On the bar caption while it has something to say.

  2. Split Cell into a shelf cell.

    Inside each tile, a GridLayout of three columns (one under 600 px of tile, with each cell captioned). The center column of On demand stacks its two halves, captioned from zoneNames: "Above Clock" and "Below Clock" on a side bar, "Left of Clock" and "Right of Clock" on a top or bottom bar.

  3. Draw every widget at one size.

    On the bar: the snapshot at 1:1 in a slot as thick as the bar (bar.barSize), spaced max(2, widgetSpacing), aligned the way the zone aligns; drop fit and the Flow scale. On demand and Hidden: the snapshot at 0.8 (or WidgetIcon) with preferences.title. Use cached captures for widgets that are not on the bar now (cachedImageUrl), so names are not the only cue. Hidden keeps its 0.62 opacity.

  4. Remove the wallpaper strip and the inspector preview.

    Delete the BarBackdrop and BorderSurface behind the bar row and the one in Inspector. If powerbarForeground makes the bar's ink fall under 3:1 against Color.popups.background, give faces a backing in the other colour of AppearanceModel.textPair.

  5. Turn Inspector into a tile.

    A PrefsTile titled with the widget's name, span 12; inside, a GridLayout of 4, 2 or 1 columns: the face with its id; Zone and Visibility (PrefsChoice, compact, stacked); Show on activity and Keep at screen center (PrefsToggle, compact; they dim with their reason instead of hiding); Group (Dropdown) or, for a group, its members and Edit group.

  6. Use the bar's drop outline.

    Replace the accent placeholder tile with DropOutline's look (1 px of the foreground at 45%, the highlight radius). The held widget leaves its place as it does on the bar, so the page drags the way the bar and drawers do.

  7. Name zones by where they are.

    On a side bar, Left, Center and Right read Top, Center and Bottom everywhere on the page (shelf heads, the Zone chips, empty-cell text). shell.json keeps left, center and right.

  8. Keys and audit.

    Each shelf takes Tab focus and shares cursorCell; focusing a shelf by Tab moves the cursor to that shelf, same zone. Add the Layout page to the bench audit (sliders scenario): no scroll at 1180 × 790, every on-bar face at scale 1, no elided names.

  9. Check on the desktop.

    qs ipc -p /usr/share/omarchy/shell call powerbar preferencesPage 1, then walk it with only the keyboard and then only the pointer, with your right-edge bar and once on the top edge.

What the research says

The references this proposal borrows from, and the part each one contributed.

  • Ice, menu bar managerIts Menu Bar Layout pane shows Visible, Hidden and Always-hidden as rows of the real icons, dragged between rows. The shelf model.
  • Bartender 5, Menu Bar ItemsThe same three rows (shown, hidden, always hidden), drag up and down between them. Confirms rows per state as the familiar model.
  • KDE UserBase, Plasma panelsPanel edit mode sorts widgets by dragging them on the panel itself; widgets align top on a vertical panel. Zones read in the bar's own order.
  • Atlassian, Drag and drop design guidelinesEmpty containers show a background change, not a line; drag previews stay small and simple. Powerbar's bar already follows its own drop outline, which the page adopts instead of Atlassian's line.
  • Atlassian, Drag and drop accessibilityEvery drag needs a non-drag way to the same result; in their testing, action menus beat arrow-key moving. Why Zone and Visibility stay in the settings tile.
  • Cloudscape, In-context actionsActions on one resource sit with that resource; a split panel is for detail you inspect. Three settings are actions, not detail, so they get a tile, not a side panel.
  • Windows 11, Other system tray iconsShows tray visibility as one switch per icon, with no picture of the tray. The counterexample: without the icons' faces, people match names to glyphs.
  • Hyprland wiki, Variablesgaps_in and rounding: the tile gap (2 × gaps_in) and corners the page shares with the Bar page.
