Along the bar, across into it

Super+B puts a keyboard cursor on the clock. From there the keys that run along your bar move along it, and the keys that point across it go into a group's drawer and back, whichever edge the bar is on. Tab jumps a zone, / finds a widget by any word of its name, Enter opens it, and Esc steps back one layer at a time. The demo below is your own bar, on the right edge of your screen with your wallpaper, built from your layout and widget images.

One meaning per key, on every edge

Keys are named by direction relative to the bar. A drawer always opens into the screen, so the key that points into the screen enters it, and the opposite key comes back. Up and Down can't do that job on a side bar: they are the keys that move along it.

Everywhere: Enter or Space opens the widget under the cursor (a group: its drawer). / finds, ↓ or Tab goes to the next match. ? lists every widget's own shortcut. Esc closes the innermost thing first: the search, a drawer, a panel, then the cursor itself. Super+B again leaves at once.

Fewest presses from the clock to put the cursor on each of your 36 widgets and groups, counted by a breadth-first search over the keys each way allows. On-demand widgets count the walk through their dots, group members the step into the drawer.

What you would type to find each one

The start of a word of its name, after /; +↓ counts the matches ahead of it. A word that starts with the text beats a word that contains it, then bar order. Two items share a name (the Audio group and the Audio widget in it): the second is one ↓ away.

Motion

Moving the cursor is something you do dozens of times a minute, so it never animates: the ring is where the key put it, at once. Things that appear (the legend, a drawer, a panel) use the bar's existing timing and switch off with Animations (powerbarAnimations).

Open questions for your review

  1. Bind Super+B on install, or offer it?

    Super+B is free on your machine and in stock Omarchy (the browser is on Super+Shift+B). Binding it at install writes powerbar.lua before you asked for anything. Recommended: offer it. The Keyboard tile shows "Super B, suggested" and Enter binds it.

  2. Legend always, or only until you have used it a few times?

    Recommended: always, small, at the far edge. ? opens the full list, and the switch turns the strip off.

  3. Should hidden widgets be findable?

    Recommended: no. They have no place on the bar to put a cursor on. Preferences already finds them.

How it would be built

After you approve the draft. Everything here reuses parts Powerbar already has.

  1. A pure model, BarKeysModel.js.

    This page's nav.js, grown up: stops from the bar's slots (widgets, group headers, dots, a folded zone's drawer button), the edge's key map, and every key as a state change. Node tests replay the tables on this page.

  2. A focus mode in Bar.qml.

    An IPC omarchy-shell powerbar keys toggles it on the focused monitor's bar. While it lasts, the bar window takes exclusive keyboard focus (WlrKeyboardFocus.Exclusive), and the cursor draws with the existing keyboard highlight (HighlightModel, focus fill and accent ring). autoHideHeld includes it, so an autohidden or switched-off bar comes out the way the pointer brings it.

  3. Reuse what already moves.

    Dots open through inlineEnter. Groups open through managedDrawer.openGroup(id, slot, true), whose keyboard mode already walks members. Enter calls the slot's activate. Panels open with summonBarWidget; Omarchy's Tab-between-panels then moves the bar's cursor with it.

  4. The legend and name card.

    A small layer surface at the far edge, plus the bar's tooltip with the widget's name and its shortcut from ShortcutStore. ? lists every shortcut. The search field lives in the legend.

  5. The shortcut.

    A bar:keys target in powerbarShortcuts, written to powerbar.lua like the widget shortcuts.

  6. Check it live.

    Every edge; autohide and bar off; a held key sweeping past both groups; Tab through the dots; /au; a panel open when Super+B arrives; two monitors.

What the research says

Each source changed a rule or confirmed one.

  • Windows 11: Win+B, Win+TWin+B puts focus on the tray; B for bar is a familiar letter. Arrows move, Enter opens.
  • Taskbar with the keyboardHome and End jump to the ends; a letter jumps to a name. Here / does that, since h, j, k and l move.
  • Mac keyboard shortcutsCtrl+F8 focuses the status menus, then arrows and Return. One key to enter, arrows inside.
  • GNOME: Ctrl+Alt+Tab to the top barFocus stays on the bar until you leave. Its regression (focus dropping on release) is the case to avoid.
  • WAI-ARIA toolbar patternArrows inside, Home and End to the ends, wrapping optional. Tab jumps by zone here, since the bar has three.
  • WAI-ARIA menubarThe key that points at a submenu opens it. That is the across rule.
  • Vimium/ to find, Esc to cancel, n and N between matches. Vim users already know these keys.
  • Omarchy's PanelKeyCatcher and KeyboardPanelPanels already use h, j, k, l, Enter, Space and Esc, and Tab moves to the next panel. The bar keeps the same keys.
  • Your Hyprland input settingsRepeat 40 a second after 250 ms. No fixed delay separates a held key from a press, so groups unfold when the key is released.
  • Emil Kowalski, via the Omarchy UX skillFrequent keyboard actions don't animate. Things that appear use 110–180 ms ease-out.
